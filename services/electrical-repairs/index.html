A symptom is a starting point, not a diagnosis

Loss of power, flickering, recurring protective-device trips or a damaged device can have different causes. The useful information is what happened, where and when, not a guess made from equipment age. A Licensed Electrical Contractor should assess the installation within a defined diagnostic scope and explain the identified cause before repair work is approved.

Prepare the scope for your Alliston property

Write down the date you left, the date you returned and what was running while you were away, such as a freezer, a workshop tool charger or a home office rack. Note any smell, heat or marks without touching anything.

Ask the contractor what can be checked safely before the supply is restored to affected areas, and who should be present. Ask whether a leak, pests or damage while you were away should change the scope.

What should the assessment establish?

What symptoms were noted on returning to the empty Alliston property? The report works from that record, not from a guess about age or cause, and lists what was examined, what was found and what remains unknown. Can equipment such as a freezer or workshop device left running during the absence be used again safely? A finding on each such item answers that. Who is allowed to authorize repairs while the owner is away? That person is named. Diagnosis and repair are kept apart in the scope, so nothing is replaced before the identified cause is explained.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Should I reset breakers after coming back to an empty property?

Leave the breakers as you found them: do not reset one that has tripped, and never open the panel cover. Note what tripped, and any water, heat or burning smell, without touching anything, then ask a contractor to assess.

Does a leak found while I was away change the electrical assessment?

It can. Water near wiring or equipment is a reason to tell the contractor before anyone enters the area, because the assessment may need a different scope. Do not touch wet equipment yourself.
