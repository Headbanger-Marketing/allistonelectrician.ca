A panel and an electrical service are different parts of the system

The panel distributes electricity to property circuits. The service is the incoming supply and associated equipment. Replacing a panel does not automatically increase the available supply. A Licensed Electrical Contractor should assess existing condition and demand, planned loads and the utility requirements before recommending equipment. Spare breaker spaces alone do not establish capacity.

Prepare the scope for your Alliston property

Collect the inspection report page or insurer letter that names the concern, and take photographs of the panel from outside, labels included. Note which workshop or office equipment runs on which labelled breaker.

Ask the contractor whether the letter points at the panel equipment itself or at the load placed on it, and what the written finding should say for the insurer. Ask how your business equipment changes the answer.

What should the assessment establish?

Whatever the inspector or insurer noted about the panel is quoted exactly, then marked as either confirmed on site or not confirmed. Where the trouble lies in a damaged or mislabelled part, it is a condition problem; where it lies in whether the supply suits the equipment you run, it is a question of load, and the two are kept apart. Any home business or hobby equipment in the Alliston house is recorded as part of that load. The scope then lists what is recommended, what is optional and what the letter or report needs in order to be closed.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Does a letter from my insurer about the panel force a replacement?

Not necessarily. A letter raises a concern that a contractor should assess before anything is recommended. Ask for a written finding that says what was examined and what, if anything, needs to change.

Will a home workshop change what a panel assessment looks at?

Yes, because long running hours and several devices on at once add load that a quiet household does not. Tell the contractor about the equipment so the assessment reflects real use, not an assumed pattern.
