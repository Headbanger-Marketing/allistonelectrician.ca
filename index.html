Before work begins

Plan for longer running hours and more equipment.

Wiring planned for one use may not suit another. In a recently built home with builder provisions, rough-in outlets and panel labels may not match what was finished, and a workshop or home office may sit on circuits chosen for ordinary use.

Where equipment runs for long hours, list each device you plan to use, how many hours a day it runs and which devices run together. Include equipment you expect to add later, so the plan is not outgrown.

How do I describe a home business load to a contractor?

Give the contractor the nameplate details or product sheets for each device, the room it will sit in and when it is used. The contractor can then judge the load; you should not size anything yourself or open the panel.

Can builder-installed outlets be used for heavy hobby gear?

Not always. Receptacles roughed in by a builder may have been placed for general use and may share a circuit with other rooms. Ask the contractor whether the intended equipment needs its own connection before you plug it in.

How should I compare quotes for a workshop or studio setup?

Have each contractor list the same equipment, hours of use and room locations in the written scope. Quotes that assume different loads cannot be compared fairly, whatever the totals say.

Who may change the wiring while a builder warranty applies?

Read the warranty documents first, because they may say who should touch the wiring and whether the builder wants notice of changes. Ask the builder to explain any unclear wording in writing, and give the contractor a copy.

What happens after I send details about my equipment?

Treat your details as an enquiry, taken in and reviewed. Use the site as background reading; it neither schedules visits nor sets prices, and performs no work. If you go on with a Licensed Electrical Contractor, have it confirm its name and licence separately before you commit, then agree the job and its cost directly, and the dates.
